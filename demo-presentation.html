Money should only move when a promise is actually kept.

Most digital deals treat "hold the money" and "release the money" as one blurry step. That's where trust breaks down — funds move too early, too late, or on someone's word instead of real proof.

"Funds are held once, reserved flexibly, verified against real proof, and released only when the deal's actual conditions are met."
Marketplace escrow is our story today - the idea applies far beyond it.
The problem

Escrow today is a black box.

Buyers don't know if their money is safe. Sellers don't know when they'll be paid. Platforms manually chase proof of delivery. And everyone is trusting a single system to get contract terms, timing, and money movement right all at once.

What goes wrong today

  • Funds get released on assumption, not verified proof.
  • Disputes happen because no one can see "why" a payment happened.
  • Every new use case needs a brand new, custom-built system.

What we believe instead

  • Holding money and deciding to release it should never be the same step.
  • Proof should be checked against the actual deal, not just "looks fine."
  • The same reservation and release pattern should work for any deal type.
This is the gap Living Ledger is built to close.
Our approach

Seven honest stages, not one black box.

Every stage of the deal is handled independently, so no single step can quietly take on someone else's responsibility.

1Agree

Capture the deal and its real condition for payment.

2Hold

Custody funds safely, independent of any one deal.

3Reserve

Earmark funds for a deal without moving them yet.

4Prove

Check real evidence against the actual obligation.

5Decide

One accountable checkpoint approves release.

6Distribute

Split the payout fairly across every stakeholder.

7Settle

Produce a real, bank-ready payment artifact.

Each stage can be explained and justified on its own.
Why it's different

We designed for trust, not just automation.

AI assists, humans decide

AI checks evidence against real obligations and gives a confidence signal - but it never has the authority to release funds. That decision always goes through one accountable checkpoint.

Timing flexibility, by design

Sometimes the deal exists before the money arrives. Sometimes the money arrives first. Our reservation logic supports both, instead of forcing one rigid order of operations.

Explainable by nature

Because every stage is separate, we can always answer "why did this payment happen?" with a clear, traceable answer - not a guess.

One pattern, many industries

Marketplace escrow, trade finance milestones, construction progress payments, insurance claims - the same reserve-verify-release pattern applies to all of them.

Closing
